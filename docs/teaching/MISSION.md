# Mission

## Why I want to learn this

I built ARES and I have to defend its numbers in public: a paper, a PPT, and
hackathon Q&A. The scoreboard my own tool prints has five figures on it and I
cannot yet explain, unprompted, why one of them being 100% is unimpressive and
another being 51.5% is the real result.

The failure mode I am trying to avoid is standing in front of a judge who asks
"so your verification is 100% accurate?" and answering in a way that either
oversells (dishonest) or undersells (throws away the actual achievement).

## What success looks like

- I can read a raw ARES run block cold and say what happened, in order.
- I can name which metric is the headline and why the other four are context.
- I can answer "isn't 100% precision suspicious?" without hedging.
- I can explain why selection recall and verified-edge recall are equal in every
  run so far, and what it would mean if they ever diverged.
- I can defend the local-vs-frontier gap without pretending it isn't a gap.

## Constraints

- Deadline-driven. Lessons must be short and immediately usable.
- Grounded in ARES's real numbers, not textbook examples. The examples should be
  runs I actually produced.
